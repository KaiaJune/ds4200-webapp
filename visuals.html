Trends By Genre & Age Group

Top Genre Popularity Among Age Groups

We begin our analysis by looking at how different age groups view music genres. Figure 1 is a radar chart showing the percentage of each age group that prefers each genre, with filters to compare across demographics.

From this chart, a few trends emerge. Teens show a stronger preference for Classical music, while those aged 46–60 have a more even distribution across genres, indicating no single dominant preference. A key pattern is the relationship between 18–25 year olds and EDM, where this group shows the strongest preference for a single genre.

Within the 18–25 group, about 14.1% of users prefer EDM, slightly higher than other age groups–genre combinations (e.g., 26–35 and Jazz at ~12.5%). Overall, the top genres by age group are Classical, EDM, Jazz, Reggae, and Classical respectively.

Key takeaway: Genre preferences vary by age with younger groups showing stronger, more concentrated preferences, while older groups are more evenly distributed across genres.

Repeat Song Rate Among Top Genres

Next, we analyze how genre and age affect repeat song rate. Figure 2 is a set of boxplots by genre, that can filter by age group, next to Figure 1 that also filters.

For teens, Classical is the top genre, with relatively high repeat rates for Classical and Hip-Hop, suggesting some alignment between preference and behavior. For ages 18–25, EDM is most preferred, but Metal has the highest repeat rate, showing that popularity and repeat behavior do not always align.

For ages 26–35, R&B has the highest repeat rates, while the 36–45 group shows higher repeat rates for Rock, Country, and EDM. The 46–60 group has similar repeat rates across genres, matching their more even preferences.

Overall, repeat song rate varies by age and genre, but not always in line with popularity. This likely reflects other factors (such as listening habits, mood, or playlist usage) influencing whether users replay songs.

Key takeaway: Genre popularity does not consistently predict repeat listening, users often replay genres that are not their top preference, likely due to differences in listening behavior rather than preference alone.

R&B listeners aged 26–35 had one of the higher median repeat song rates (~51%). Figure 3 shows a scatterplot of repeat song rate versus age within this group.

The plot shows a wide spread of repeat rates across ages 26–35, without a clear upward or downward trend. This suggests that while this group has relatively high repeat behavior overall, age within the group does not strongly influence repeat rate.

Key takeaway: Even within a high-repeat group, individual behavior varies widely and does not follow a clear age trend.

Discover Weekly Engagement Trends

Next, we can analyze how age and genre impacts the weekly engagement with the discover applications. Figure 4 is a bar chart depicting discover weekly engagement accross each genre. The graph can be filtered to any age group.

Across all age groups, engagement levels are relatively similar across genres, suggesting no strong overall trend. However, when filtering by age group, some differences appear. Teenagers who prefer Hip-Hop and Reggae show higher engagement. Hip-Hop also remains high for the 18–25 group. For 26–35, Jazz shows slightly higher engagement, while 36–45 leans toward R&B, and 46–60 shows somewhat higher engagement for Rock.

Overall, these differences are present but relatively small, which helps explain why no strong global trend appears.

Key takeaway: Weekly engagement varies slightly by age and genre, but differences are modest, there is no strong overall pattern.

There appears to be no strong relationship between genre popularity and weekly engagement, nor between repeat song rate and engagement. However, some smaller patterns exist within specific groups.

For users aged 25 and under who prefer Hip-Hop, engagement tends to be relatively high. Figure 5 shows engagement across ages within this group.

The scatterplot shows variability across ages, with no clear linear trend, but generally higher engagement levels compared to other groups. This suggests that while age alone does not drive engagement, this specific subgroup tends to engage more overall.

Key takeaway: Certain subgroups (like younger Hip-Hop listeners) show higher engagement, even without a clear age-based trend.

Streaming Time of Day by Genre

Next, we analyze how streaming activity varies across genres and times of day. Figure 6 is a heatmap showing morning, afternoon, and night on the x-axis and genres on the y-axis.

Each cell represents the percentage of listeners within that genre who are most active during that time period, with darker shading indicating a higher concentration of listeners.

Nighttime appears to be slightly more dominant across many genres, suggesting users are more active during leisure hours. However, differences between time periods are not extreme, indicating fairly balanced listening behavior overall.

When filtering by age group, younger users tend to show slightly more concentrated nighttime listening, while older groups appear more evenly distributed throughout the day.

Key takeaway: Listening is somewhat higher at night, especially for younger users, but overall usage is fairly balanced across the day.

Analyzing Geographic Location & Genre Preferences

Top Genres by Country

Finally, we analyze how geographic location relates to genre preferences. Figure 7 is a choropleth map showing genre popularity across countries.

The map shows variation between countries, though differences are generally moderate rather than extreme. For example, Germany shows a wider spread across genres compared to others, while some genres like Country remain relatively consistent across locations.

Some nearby countries (such as the U.S. and Canada) show similarities in certain genres like EDM and Rock, but differ in others, suggesting that geographic proximity alone does not fully explain preferences. Overall, variation exists across countries, but no single strong geographic pattern dominates.

Key takeaway: Geography influences genre preferences slightly, but differences are moderate and not strongly clustered.
