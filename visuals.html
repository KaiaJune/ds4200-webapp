Trends By Genre & Age Group

Top Genre Popularity Among Age Groups

We begin our analysis by looking at how different age groups view music genres. Figure 1 is a radar chart that displays the percentage of each age group that prefers each genre. The chart can be filtered to five different age groups. With each filter, the relationship for each genre is shown.

From this chart, we can determine a few trends: teens have a strong affinity for classical music. Those aged 46-60 have the most even split of top genres. However, what stands out from this chart is the relationship between 18-25 year olds and EDM music. The chart shows their relationship is the strongest compared to all other groups.

Inside the 18-25 age group, 14.1% of users had EDM as their top genre. This is a higher percentage than any other age group & top genre pair, with the next highest being 12.5% of those aged 26-35 liking Jazz music.

Overall, the top genres per age group (Teen, 18-25, 26-35, 36-45, 46-60) are Classical, EDM, Jazz, Reggae, and Classical, respectively.

Repeat Song Rate Among Top Genres

Next, we can analyze how genre and age affects repeat song rate. Figure 2 is an array of boxplots, each corresponding to a different genre. The chart can be filtered to any age group. We can compare this chart to Figure 1.

We begin by analyzing teenagers. Their top genre was Classical music. Of all of the genres, teenagers had the highest median repeat song rate for Hip-Hop and Classical music. This aligns with how Teens tend to most enjoy Classical music, as per Figure 2. Next, looking at those aged 18-25, their top genre was EDM and they had a very strong relationship. Despite this, the median repeat song rate was highest in this age group for Metal music. Those aged 26-35 most preferred Jazz and R&B music. The highest median repeat song rate is for R&B. For the 36-45 age group, they had the highest repeat song rate for rock, country and Edm. Rock was this age group's second most popular genre. And finally, the 46-60 age group had similar repeat song rates accross the board. This aligns with how they didn't have a clear top genre.

From this analysis, it is clear that repeat song rate depends on the genre and age group of the listeners. There is some similarities between repeat song rate and genre popularity, but there is no clear and definitive trend. However, some genres and age groups showed a strong repeat song rate. R&B listeners aged 26-35 had the highest overall median repeat song rate at 51.26%. Figure 3 is a scatterplot showing age and repeat song rate within this age group for R&B music.

Discover Weekly Engagement Trends

Next, we can analyze how age and genre impacts the weekly engagement with the discover applications. Figure 4 is a bar chart depicting discover weekly engagement accross each genre. The graph can be filtered to any age group.

When looking at the discover weekly engagement trends accross all age groups, there is no significant trend. However, when filtering to specific age groups, some trends appear. First looking at teenagers, those who prefer Hip-Hop and Reggae have the highest weekly engagement. Hip-hop continues to have the highest weekly engagement for the 18-25 age group. Those aged 26-35 that liked Jazz had the highest engagement. R&B was the highest for 36-45 year olds. And finally, the 46-60 age group had the highest engagement among those who like Rock music.

There appears to be no strong correlation between more popular genres and higher daily weekly engagement. There also seems to be no connection between repeat song rate and daily weekly engagement. However, within specific age groups there are genres with higher engagement. The biggest trend comes from those aged 25 and under who prefer Hip-Hop music: they use the discover weekly most often. Figure 5 displays this idea. It shows discover weekly engagement accross ages within the 18-25 age group.

Streaming Time of Day by Genre

Next, we analyze how streaming activity varies across music genres and times of day, while also allowing the chart to be filtered by age group. Figure 7 is an interactive heatmap that shows morning, afternoon, and night on the x axis, and the top genres on the y axis.

Each cell represents the percentage of listeners within that genre who are most active during that time period, with darker shading indicating a higher concentration of listeners.

From this chart, we can identify several trends. Nighttime appears to be the most dominant listening period across many genres, suggesting that users are more active during leisure hours such as after school or work. Some genres show more concentrated listening at specific times of day, while others are more evenly distributed, indicating differences in how and when music is consumed.

When filtering by age group, we see that listening patterns shift across demographics. Younger users tend to have more concentrated listening behavior, often skewing toward nighttime, while older age groups show more balanced patterns throughout the day. Overall, this visualization highlights that streaming behavior is shaped not only by genre preference, but also by time of day and age, adding another dimension to understanding global music listening trends.

Analyzing Geographic Location & Genre Preferences

Top Genres by Country

Next, we can analyze if geographic location has any effect on genre. Figure 6 is a chloropleth map, which highlights the popularity of genres accross different countries.

This map highlights several trends. Most notably Germany shows the widest variation in genre preferences among all countries, with only 7% of users listening to Reggae as their top genre, while 13.6% prefer EDM.

Pop shows the greatest variation in genre, with a 5.9% difference between its highest and lowest values, followed by EDM (5.5% difference) and then Hip-Hop (5.49% difference). While Country music has the smallest range, at only 2.8% difference.

In the United States, Reggae and EDM are the most popular genres, while the least popular are R&B and Hip-Hop.

We also explored how geographic proximity may influence top genre preferences. For EDM, Canada and the United States have the same percentage, at 11.2%, and for Rock with 9.8% for the United States and 9.5% for Canada. However, they are very different for other genres. For example, for metal the United States is 10.7% but Canada is 7.7%. The overall strongest proximity-based cluster overall is UK, France and Germany, especially for R&B, Metal, and Classical.
