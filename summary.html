Summary & Conclusions

This page provides an overview of key findings from the global streaming trends analysis and what can be done in the future.

Music preferences vary significantly by both age and geographic location, but popularity alone does not reliably predict user behavior. Across multiple analyses, there is no consistent relationship between a genre’s popularity and key engagement metrics such as repeat listening or weekly interaction. In many cases, genres with the largest listener base are not the ones that generate the highest levels of engagement, indicating that user behavior is driven more by genre-specific characteristics than overall popularity.

Age plays a critical role in shaping these patterns. Younger audiences, particularly those under 25, exhibit the strongest alignment with specific genres and the highest levels of engagement. This group consistently drives discovery and interaction, making them the most influential for identifying emerging trends and amplifying new music. In contrast, older age groups display more balanced genre preferences and less concentrated engagement patterns, suggesting a more diversified listening behavior.

Geographic patterns further reinforce the complexity of music preferences. While proximity can contribute to similarities between nearby countries, it does not fully determine listening behavior. Significant variation exists even among geographically close regions, indicating that cultural and regional factors play an important role. Additionally, some genres demonstrate relatively consistent performance across countries, while others show substantial variation, suggesting that certain genres have broader global appeal while others are more regionally dependent.

These findings have clear implications for the music industry. Effective marketing, distribution, and touring strategies should be tailored to specific audience segments rather than relying on overall popularity trends. Aligning strategies with age-based engagement patterns and regional preferences can lead to more targeted outreach, stronger audience connection, and more efficient allocation of resources.

Recommendations

EDM

  • Focus heavily on ages 18–25, where EDM has the highest top-genre percentage (~14%) across all groups
  • Prioritize high-engagement platforms (Spotify playlists, TikTok, festival content) to match strong youth interaction patterns
  • Target regions with consistent EDM performance, including:
    • United States
    • Canada
    • United Kingdom
    • Germany
  • Leverage festival culture + live events, where EDM audiences are especially active

Hip-Hop

  • Target users under 25, where Hip-Hop shows the highest weekly engagement levels
  • Focus on discovery-driven platforms (e.g., algorithmic playlists, short-form video)
  • Use frequent releases and trend-based drops to align with high engagement behavior
  • Emphasize virality + social sharing, since engagement, not repeat rate, is the key strength

Country

  • Focus on high repeat listening behavior, especially among ages 26–45
  • Build long-term artist loyalty rather than short-term spikes
  • Invest in:
    • curated playlists
    • consistent release schedules
  • Target markets where repeat behavior is strong, especially in the United States

Metal

  • Target niche but highly engaged audiences with consistently high repeat song rates
  • Focus on community-building:
    • fan forums
    • live shows
    • dedicated platforms
  • Prioritize depth over reach, fewer listeners, but more loyal and active

Pop

  • Avoid a global “one-size-fits-all” strategy due to high variation across countries (largest range ~5.9%)
  • Customize marketing by region:
    • Different genres dominate in different countries
  • Use localized playlists, influencers, and campaigns to match regional taste differences

Touring Strategy (All Genres)

  • Group countries with similar genre patterns to optimize tours:
    • Strong cluster: United Kingdom, France, Germany
  • Align tour stops with genre strength + engagement levels
  • Focus on regions where both popularity AND engagement are high (not just one)

Older Audiences (36+)

  • Use a multi-genre strategy, since preferences are more evenly distributed
  • Avoid over-investing in a single genre
  • Focus on:
    • broad catalog promotion
    • nostalgia + familiarity-driven engagement

Future Work

Looking ahead, there are several opportunities to build on this work. Future analysis could account for potential user bias, incorporate more detailed geographic data at the regional or city level, and include additional demographic variables such as gender. Expanding the dataset and analyzing trends over time would help distinguish long-term patterns from short-term shifts. Exploring causal relationships between age, genre, and engagement would also provide deeper insight beyond simple correlations.
